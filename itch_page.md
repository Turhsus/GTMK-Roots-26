# Pack Your Child's Adventure

**You don't go on the adventure. You pack the bag.**

Your little one has decided they're an adventurer. There's a quest board in town, a road out
of it, and a backpack on the kitchen table that is smaller than everything they want to
bring. That last part is your problem.

Every item has a shape. The bread is two cells wide, the sword runs three long, the shield
eats a square corner of the bag all by itself. Drag them, turn them, wedge them together —
and then decide what gets left behind, because something always does. Food, health, combat,
utility: four bars, four quiet promises, and never enough room to keep all of them.

Then you press **Send off**, and you read what happened.

The adventure log is written by your packing, not by luck. Pack no rations for a two-day
errand and they come home hungry and honest about it. Forget the package on a delivery quest
and everyone will be polite about it in a way that stings. Leave the herbs no room to ride
home and there are no herbs. The bag never refuses a bad idea — it just tells you about it
afterward, in your child's voice.

**Failure is not a game over.** The day clock keeps running. Your little one comes back, and
you sit with them, and they learn something — a perk, a habit, a small piece of hardening
that stays with them for the rest of the run. *"Failure isn't the end, little one. It's a
lesson."*

Between quests there's the town. A day's worth of walking, a handful of coins, a grocer and a
blacksmith and an apothecary and a leatherworker who — if you've saved enough — will stitch
you a bigger bag. Spend the gold on gear, or on room. Both are the same decision wearing
different clothes.

The quests get harder. The child gets taller. Somewhere in there the packing stops being
about the items and starts being about how much you're still allowed to help.

And one morning they'll pack the bag themselves, and you'll stand at the door listing
everything they might have forgotten, and they'll say *"MOM. Quit stalling."*
